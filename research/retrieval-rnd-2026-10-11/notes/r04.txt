Research 04/25 — learned sparse retrieval (verified primary literature; 2026-10-11)

Recommendation: Preserve Lumen's exact-symbol/FTS5 lane. Prototype an OPTIONAL learned-expansion lane with separate provenance and score fusion. Best CPU-default research direction: compact document-side semantic expansion computed once per changed symbol chunk. Best quality challenger: SPLADE-Code-0.6B with query/document encoding and pruned sparse retrieval. Evidence supports this experiment, not declaring either a verified Lumen win.

1) SPLADE v2: Sparse Lexical and Expansion Model for Information Retrieval — Formal et al., 2021-09-21. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.10086
Verified: SPLADE produces sparse weighted vocabulary dimensions, including absent-but-related words, then scores a sparse dot product. Document-only SPLADE-doc eliminates neural query encoding. On MS MARCO dev, Table 1 reports MRR@10: BM25 .184; SPLADE-doc .322; SPLADE-max .340; distilled SPLADE-max .368. The document-only result demonstrates that semantic retrieval need not require online neural inference. A heavily sparse model with 19 nonzero terms/document still reached .296 MRR@10. Indexing uses one forward pass/document, versus repeated generation for doc2query-T5. FLOPS regularization manages frequent postings and speed, not simply vector cardinality. Caveats: natural-language passages, maximum 256 tokens, nonidentical training for document-only configuration; neither code evidence nor modern laptop timing. More expansion increases postings/storage, but exact byte comparisons were not provided.

2) Learning Passage Impacts for Inverted Indexes (DeepImpact) — Mallia et al., 2021-04-24 / SIGIR 2021. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12016
Verified: generate likely queries for each document (DocT5Query), then learn document term impact weights. Queries need no transformer. Weights quantize to eight bits, with no observed precision loss in their experiment. MS MARCO dev MRR@10 improved from BM25 .188 to .326; Recall@10 from .394 to .584. But first-stage mean time worsened from 13.24 to 58.64 ms on one Xeon core! Learned impacts impaired MaxScore's pruning assumptions. Its advertised approximately fivefold efficiency win was against a particular ColBERT end-to-end pipeline, not against BM25. Forty generated queries/passage plus BERT inference creates material indexing cost. This is strong evidence that fewer agent candidate reads may outweigh retrieval milliseconds, and that “inverted index compatible” does not automatically mean faster.

3) A Few Brief Notes on DeepImpact, COIL, and a Conceptual Framework for Information Retrieval Techniques — Lin and Ma, 2021-06-28. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14807
Verified: uniCOIL reduces contextual token vectors to scalar nonnegative weights; query/document weights quantize to eight bits and work with Lucene impact indexes. MS MARCO MRR@10: uniCOIL without expansion .315; with doc2query-T5 .352. This isolates useful expansion benefit. Unlike DeepImpact, query weights require a model forward pass. Separate term weighting from expansion in experiments: a better weighting model cannot recover documents that share no retained query dimensions. Evidence is passage retrieval, not identifier search or agent task completion; no reliable CPU end-to-end latency comparison is supplied.

4) On the Challenges and Opportunities of Learned Sparse Retrieval for Code — Lupart et al., 2026-03-23 preprint. https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22008v1
Verified: SPLADE-Code-0.6B scores 75.4 MTEB-Code nDCG@10, versus BM25 47.9. Controlled 8B results: lexical weighting without expansion 45.8; expanded sparse 77.8; dense 75.6. Thus expansion is central, not decoration. Their fast cascade retrieves using top-(10 query,100 document) dimensions then rescores with (500,1000); below-1-ms search on 1M passages uses Seismic and a single AMD EPYC processor. This is index-search evidence, not proof of sub-ms model encoding. The paper acknowledges absent agent end-to-end validation and repository-distribution gaps. 0.6B–8B model inference and corpus encoding are materially heavier than BM25; strongest sparse results are not uniformly best across datasets. Source tables distinguish CodeRAG and CPRet averages; accompanying prose sometimes reverses their names, so cite tables.

Lumen hypothesis and falsification:
A separate cached expansion field or sparse sidecar avoids corrupting raw identifier precision. FTS5 BM25 over generated terms is an approximation to learned dot-product ranking, not SPLADE itself. Query-only expansion avoids corpus inference/storage but pays each query and risks intent drift; document-only amortizes work but may miss rare new intent. Test both against full bidirectional sparse retrieval and BM25 on identical chunks. Fix 2K/4K evidence-token budgets; measure relevant-span recall, downstream solve rate, tool calls, p50/p95 total latency including encoding, update lag, bytes/chunk, and cold-start cost. Stratify exact symbols, paraphrases, behavior-only queries, errors, cross-language and no-answer queries; use held-out repositories. Ablate expansion versus learned weights, doc-only versus query-only, fusion, and pruning. Reject the added lane unless paraphrase/behavior gains survive paired confidence intervals without meaningful exact-symbol or total task-cost regression. No published result here establishes superiority to rg for exhaustive matching.
