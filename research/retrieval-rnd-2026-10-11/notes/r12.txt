R12 — Reranking and fusion; literature verified 2026-10-11

Recommendation: use inexpensive fusion as the default, evaluate a small local cross-encoder on a bounded shortlist, and reserve agent/LLM reranking for ambiguous queries. This is an engineering hypothesis, not a demonstrated Lumen result. Reranking cannot recover a relevant document absent from its input; improve candidate recall before buying a stronger reranker.

Primary evidence

1. Cormack, Clarke, Büttcher, “Reciprocal Rank Fusion outperforms Condorcet and individual Rank Learning Methods,” SIGIR, July 2009.
https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~gvcormac/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf
Rank-only RRF combines heterogeneous retrievers without score calibration or training labels: sum 1/(k+rank), originally k=60. Original TREC experiments reported average improvements around 4–5% over alternatives; this is old ad-hoc IR evidence, not code retrieval or a universal guarantee. Useful initial baseline when lexical, symbol, graph, and semantic scores have unrelated scales.

2. Bruch, Gai, Ingber, “An Analysis of Fusion Functions for Hybrid Retrieval,” first submitted October 21, 2022; ACM TOIS 2023.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11934
The authors directly challenge RRF’s parameter robustness: normalized lexical/semantic score convex combination beats RRF in their in-domain and out-of-domain evaluations, and a single learned mixing weight is sample efficient. This supports testing tuned score fusion once labels exist, not declaring RRF optimal. Verified primary abstract; PDF retrieval failed, so no granular benchmark numbers claimed. Their two-retriever findings do not establish how to weight AST/graph channels or enforce logical constraints.

3. MacAvaney et al., “Efficient Document Re-Ranking for Transformers by Precomputing Term Representations,” SIGIR 2020, arXiv April 29, 2020.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14255
PreTTR shifts early document transformer layers to indexing, leaving query/document interaction to later layers. Table 5 reports 100-document reranking falling from 1.941 s to 46 ms (42.2x) on WebTrack with 11 precomputed layers. Important qualification: GTX 1080 Ti, representations already in RAM, and speedup versus unmodified BERT rather than grep. P@20 was comparable there, but ERR@20 worsened; the same split severely harmed Robust04. This is evidence for computation reuse, not a safe universal architecture or a laptop latency promise. Storage and reindexing costs also grow.

Proposed bounded cascade (parameters to validate)

• Keep explicit path, language, revision, quoted literal, and symbol constraints as machine-checked predicates; do not let a semantic score override them. Negation in natural language requires scope interpretation, not simply filtering every document containing the excluded word. Preserve original query alongside expansions and create contrastive negation tests.
• Retrieve top 100 lexical candidates plus up to 100 complementary semantic/structural candidates; deduplicate spans, fuse, rerank at most 40 chunks with a small local cross-encoder. Feed the final 8–12 diverse, source-addressable chunks within a token budget. Exact symbol/path hits can bypass neural reranking.
• If confidence is low, broaden candidate generation before optional LLM reranking of 10–20 candidates. LLM selection should emit existing IDs, retain provenance, and have a strict deadline. This escalation recommendation is not directly tested by the three papers.

Decisive ablation: fixed candidate union, compare no rerank/RRF/tuned normalized fusion/local cross-encoder/LLM; separately vary candidate counts 20/40/100 and retrieval channels. Report candidate recall ceiling, nDCG@10, evidence sufficiency per 2k/4k tokens, task success, p50/p95 latency, warm/cold cost, RAM/index size, update time, and exact/negation regressions. Split by repository and hold out query paraphrases to avoid tuning leakage. Only adopt neural reranking if its task-success gains justify total cost over stronger candidate generation.
