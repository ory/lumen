R&D RESEARCH REVIEW · 11 OCTOBER 2026 · LUMEN + GENERAL DOCUMENT RETRIEVAL

Retrieve the evidence an agent needs, within a measured budget

The strongest practical next step is query-aware hybrid retrieval with token-budgeted evidence selection. The most interesting research extension is to select complementary evidence bundles and adapt the next search action to the evidence still missing.

Decision: first improve Lumen's query-dependent ranking and global context budget; then compare lexical+dense fusion against that inexpensive baseline. Add structural expansion, reranking and stronger semantic representations only when held-out end-to-end gains justify their cost. This is an evidence-backed recommendation for experiments, not a verified universal winner.

Scope and method. Twenty-five separate AI subagents performed specialist investigations, including an independent local-code audit and skeptical review. The runtime permits three concurrent subagents, so the work ran in batches. The agents are not affiliated with a professional scientific organization. This review uses primary research papers, publication records and local source inspection; it is a broad research scan, not an exhaustive systematic review or independent reproduction of the papers' benchmarks. Publication searches include 2025–2026 work; recent preprints receive less weight than established results.

What was verified. The cited mechanisms and selected reported findings were checked against primary sources. The production dense-search path and existing index/chunking features were inspected. The local command below passed. It verifies a toy SQLite lexical/vector fusion query, not production integration, retrieval quality, latency superiority or the proposed complete system.

The recommended architecture

1 · SCOPEQuery + literals + revision2 · CANDIDATESExact / lexical / semantic3 · RELATIONSOptional bounded expansion4 · SELECTIONComplementary source spans5 · RETURNBudgeted evidence + handles6 · FEEDBACKUncovered facets or stop
  1. Preserve exactness and scope. Keep original wording, explicit symbols and quoted text. Use revision, project and path membership to define eligible evidence. Fuzzy alternatives are labeled alternatives; they do not rewrite hard literal constraints.
  2. Generate candidates cheaply. For exact lookups, prioritize direct matches. For conceptual questions, compare lexical+dense fusion against each branch alone. Use reciprocal rank fusion as a baseline, while ensuring explicit constraints are respected. Larger candidate pools cost ranking time, not necessarily reader context.
  3. Rank for the actual question. Avoid unconditional penalties for tests or documents. A small reranker is optional and must demonstrate savings after including its inference cost.
  4. Select sets of evidence. Prefer source units that address missing aspects rather than near-duplicates. Retain the decisive definition even if diversification appears attractive. Protect semantic units such as negations, operators, table headers, values and units.
  5. Expand only when needed. Follow a bounded number of authoritative document links or resolved code references. Return a small implementation/caller/test bundle when it serves the question. Use limited graph radius and candidate budgets; do not construct an expensive global LLM knowledge graph by default.
  6. Track what the agent has already received. Return source text with file/revision/span handles and offer expansion. Reuse retrieved evidence within the same snapshot, but do not suppress needed material merely because it appeared earlier in a compacted or different agent context.
  7. Control additional work. Begin with simple rules and hard budgets. Learn a controller only after logging enough actual outcomes. Its own inference and bookkeeping must count in the budget.

Illustration. For “where do retries stop when the caller cancels?”, returning several similarly named retry helpers may waste context. A better candidate evidence set could contain the retry loop, the caller's cancellation propagation, and a test of cancellation behavior. Their usefulness must be checked from source; proximity in a graph or high similarity alone does not prove the behavior.

The research hypothesis, separated from established components

Hypothesis: a snapshot-aware selector of complementary evidence bundles, coupled to a low-overhead search controller, will reduce agent rounds and total tokens at matched task success. Its ingredients draw from information retrieval, program analysis, submodular optimization, database optimization, incremental build systems and decision-theoretic metareasoning. This synthesis is a proposed Lumen experiment; scientific originality has not been established.

Evidence-selection surrogate:
  F(S) = sum over query aspects a of w[a] * max over spans d in S of support[a,d]
  subject to total returned tokens <= B

Next-action heuristic:
  estimated improvement in final task success
  - lambda * additional latency
  - mu * additional model/context cost

With fixed nonnegative weights and support scores, the coverage surrogate is monotone submodular. Real evidence utility can violate that assumption: two passages may be useful only together. Enumerating a small number of source-linked bundles is one experiment, but shared-span deduplication changes costs and does not automatically preserve a textbook approximation guarantee. Estimates of support and success gain require held-out calibration; the formula is not a correctness proof. Submodular knapsack · Selecting Computations

Most interesting individual algorithmic candidate: MUVERA's randomized reduction of multi-vector retrieval to fixed-dimensional candidate search. Test it if late interaction gives valuable quality improvements but its candidate stage becomes a bottleneck. This is distinct from recommending it as the first local implementation. MUVERA

Pilot		Filtered doc2query moves intent expansion to ingestion; synthetic questions must be filtered and stored separately from evidence. 6Adaptive retrieval control
Build baseline		Adaptive-RAG and EfficientRAG support selective escalation and lightweight iteration. Start with rules and hard budgets; learn routing only after collecting outcome data. Score gaps and model confidence are features, not calibrated proof that evidence is sufficient.

7Set coverage and evidence selection
Priority experiment		Select complementary source units under a token budget instead of simply taking top-k. MMR is the cheap baseline. Weighted maximum facet coverage is a useful submodular surrogate; complete multi-hop chains can have increasing returns, so bounded evidence bundles need separate treatment. Naive marginal-gain-per-token greedy does not inherit the classical knapsack guarantee.

MMR · Submodular knapsack

8Metareasoning and value of computation
Priority experiment		Choose the next search action according to predicted improvement in the final task minus time and token costs. Metareasoning better describes within-query search than ordinary reward-collecting bandits. MBA-RAG supports adaptive strategy selection; calibration, logged-policy bias, and useful two-step searches remain concerns.

Selecting Computations · MBA-RAG

9Program analysis and dependency slicing
Pilot		RepoGraph supports source-structure-assisted repair; code property graphs supply richer dependency representations. Use semantic anchors, then small caller/callee/test neighborhoods. AST call syntax does not resolve targets by itself: use compiler/type information when available and label heuristic edges.

RepoGraph · Code Property Graphs

10Incremental builds and semantic caches
Build on existing		Reuse content artifacts by input and pipeline hashes, while keeping current paths and corpus membership separate. Contextual embeddings depend on their whole encoding window. A cached hit remaining unchanged is insufficient: a newly added document can outrank it. Result caches need corpus-generation validity or correct changed-partition reconciliation.

Build Systems a la Carte · Semantic Data Caching

11Adaptive dimensions and ANN
Scale-dependent		Matryoshka embeddings support small-prefix retrieval and full-vector reranking; ordinary embeddings cannot simply be truncated with the same expectation. Score-aware quantization preserves ranking-relevant information. Keep exact scans as the baseline for small corpora and measure index/update overhead before introducing ANN.

Matryoshka · Score-aware quantization

12Fuzzy lexical search and fusion
Build first		Trigram filtering plus exact verification speeds selective regex search; bounded edit-distance lookup handles misspelled identifiers. Index raw symbols, case/snake components and paths separately. Reciprocal rank fusion combines ranked channels but does not guarantee that exact matches remain first; explicit literal constraints need their own policy.

Indexed regular-expression search · Reciprocal Rank Fusion

13Context compression and progressive reads
Build carefully		RECOMP and LLMLingua-2 show that context reduction can save work. For source code and precise documents, prefer whole evidence spans with expandable handles. Protect negations, operators, values, units, and headers. Count compression and follow-up reads in total cost; token deletion is not a semantic-preservation guarantee.

RECOMP · LLMLingua-2

14Visual documents and tables
Specialist branch		ColPali/ColQwen retrieve page images using patch-level late interaction, retaining layout lost in text extraction. Use this selectively for figures, scans and tables. Retrieve the relevant table, then verify cells, units and dates and execute numeric predicates deterministically. Page or crop provenance is essential.

ColPali · Dense table retrieval

15Time, versions, negation and predicates
Build constraints		Separate hard metadata predicates from approximate relevance. Apply current revision and path filters before ranking; temporal validity is not synonymous with publication time. NevIR demonstrates negation failures. Numeric constraints require typed values and units; semantic negation cannot be reduced to absence of a keyword.

ACORN · NevIR

16Reasoning-trained retrieval
Pilot after baseline		ReasonIR learns difficult relevance distinctions. BRIGHT-Pro evaluates complementary evidence in agents: its 175-query agentic experiment shows that static retriever rankings and final answer quality can diverge. These results motivate evidence coverage and search-round measurements, not selecting the highest static score regardless of cost.

ReasonIR · BRIGHT-Pro / RTriever

17Generative document addressing
Defer replacement		Generating document IDs is an alternative to searching an explicit index. IncDSI and dynamic-corpus studies show that updates are possible, so blanket claims of impossible updates are wrong. For Lumen, borrowing semantic aliases while retaining an incrementally maintained source index is a lower-risk experiment than replacing the retrieval engine.

IncDSI · Generative retrieval on dynamic corpora

18RL-trained search policies
Defer training		Search-R1 learns interleaved retrieval but can search more and generate longer responses; accuracy rewards alone do not minimize cost. AnySearch explicitly studies variable budgets. CodeScout supplies an important terminal-only code-localization challenger. Test better routing before training a new agent.

Search-R1 · CodeScout

19Database query optimization
Priority experiment		Cascades suggests plan alternatives and cost limits; Eddies suggests adapting after observed results. Transfer these at retrieval-action boundaries. Lexical, dense and graph actions are not semantically equivalent database plans: a cheaper nonempty result cannot justify assuming no evidence was missed.

Cascades · Eddies

20Domain adaptation and hard negatives
Later optimization		GPL, InPars and CoRNStack support offline query synthesis, teacher distillation and code-specific negatives. This can amortize reasoning into a smaller retriever. Mine failures only after establishing a baseline; wrappers and related functions may be additional positives rather than negatives. Hold out repositories and time periods, and prevent clone leakage.

21Evaluation and causal attribution
Required		Evaluate identical agents with competent iterative rg, lexical, dense, fixed hybrid and proposed extensions. Measure task success and total cost, evidence recall at fixed tokens, p95 latency, updates and cold indexing. Use held-out repositories, repeated paired trials and clustered uncertainty. Lumen's small published task set and LLM judging motivate broader testing; output tokens alone do not measure retrieved context.

Build evidence fidelity		Keep competing interpretations and contradictory source spans separate. A similarity score or repeated agreement is not truth. Verification plan and rejection criteria

Incumbents. Compare a competent agent using iterative rg and file reads; current Lumen; current Lumen with query-aware ranking and a shared evidence budget; lexical-only; dense-only; lexical+dense fusion; and the proposed extensions. The simple improved-Lumen system is a mandatory challenger, not a deliberately weak baseline. Preserve the same agent, prompts, source snapshot and resource budget across comparisons.

Tasks. Pilot a held-out, human-reviewed set spanning exact identifiers, typos, paraphrases, symptoms without implementation names, cross-file chains, documents/configs, time/version constraints and no-answer cases. A planning range of 200–400 tasks is reasonable, but use pilot paired variance and a predeclared minimum meaningful effect to determine sample size. Include entire held-out repositories and query families; repeated runs do not become independent new tasks.

Primary outcome. Report executable task success for code changes and blinded supported-answer correctness for documents. Also report total spend divided by successful tasks, together with success rate and per-task paired differences. A cheaper run that fails is not a saving in successful work. Use task/repository-clustered uncertainty intervals and randomize paired run order.

Context and latency. Measure evidence recall and complete-chain recall at fixed token budgets; unique and repeated evidence tokens; cumulative model-input tokens including cache treatment; peak context; output tokens; tool rounds; cold and warm end-to-end p50/p95 latency; index size; peak memory; initial build and update costs; stale or wrong-revision citations. Include embedding, rewriting, reranking, controller, compression and subsequent expansion costs.

Amortized cost per task =
  share of initial indexing and update cost
  + all retrieval / embedding / reranking / control cost
  + reader input, cached input and output cost
  + retries and follow-up reads

Ablations. Independently test query-aware priors, global token budget, lexical fusion, reranking, graph bundles, session deduplication, query rewriting and adaptive stopping. Sweep candidate and context budgets instead of choosing favorable settings per method. Identify whether gains come from better retrieval, better packaging or a stronger agent. Evaluate both cold-start and repeated-use break-even points.

Freshness and leakage. Replay additions, modifications, deletions, renames, branch switches and encoder upgrades. Compare incremental results with fresh rebuilds. Exclude gold patches, future documentation and post-fix evidence; deduplicate clones across training/test partitions. Do not remove failed runs or runs where the agent did not choose Lumen. Report failures and all-run quality rather than the best repetition.

Reject an added component when its benefit disappears after total-cost accounting, when it introduces material exact-match or freshness regressions, or when its apparent advantage does not survive paired held-out evaluation. Controller overhead, wrong inferred aspects, distracting graph neighbors and context-selection mistakes are first-class failure modes.

Implementation order justified by the evidence

  1. First: improve query-dependent ranking, establish a global evidence budget, and make all-run benchmark reporting reliable. These changes target concrete observed gaps.
  2. Next: production lexical+dense candidate fusion with explicit literal/scope handling. Compare it against the first-stage incumbent before adding other machinery.
  3. Then: complementary evidence selection and small authoritative relationship bundles; evaluate optional reranking separately.
  4. Only after measured failures: test SPLADE, corpus-conditioned embeddings or ColBERT. Use MUVERA/ANN when profiling identifies a candidate-search bottleneck. Use document expansion or domain adaptation when query volume amortizes ingestion/training cost.
  5. Specialized branches: visual/table retrieval for rich documents, hierarchy for global synthesis, and carefully budgeted multi-hop search for difficult cases. Keep expensive generative indexing and full agent RL as research alternatives rather than default dependencies.

The best-supported decision today is an experimental roadmap with strong cheap challengers. The research supports the mechanisms and tradeoffs; a claim of lower cost or better task success for the complete Lumen design awaits the proposed comparison.
